I’d verify these items in this order:

---

2. **Container and hardware match**

 Check that the waste drum is actually the specified **closed-top metal 1A1** type, that the spill pallet is in place, and that the **bungs have gaskets**.

3. **Labeling in the area**

 See whether the current labels already say exactly:

 **“Non-Regulated Waste”**
 **“Waste Polyamic Resin Solution”**
 plus shipper/address if that’s being done at point of generation.

4. **How operators really add waste**

 The doc says containers can be drained through an **open funnel** because it’s non-hazardous. Verify that this is what’s physically happening and that it’s being done cleanly and consistently.

5. **Fill limit control**

 Make sure there’s a practical way to judge the **3-inch headspace** rule. If not, that needs clarifying before training.

6. **Torque requirement**

 Check whether the area actually has a tool/method to torque bungs to **20 ft-lbs**. If nobody measures torque today, that’s a real feedback item.

7. **Timing responsibilities**

 Confirm who is actually responsible for:

 moving full drums within **3 calendar days** and
 scheduling pickup within **2 months**.

 New docs often name roles, but the real handoff is fuzzier on the floor.

8. **Logbook and BOL records**

 Verify there is already a logbook or form for **daily weight/volume**, and that someone is truly recording **full drum weight** for the BOL.

What I’d look for as feedback before approval:

🧩 steps that are technically correct but not how the work is really done 
🧰 required tools/PPE that aren’t actually present at point of use 
📝 vague ownership — especially around moving drums, weighing, and pickup scheduling 

⚠️ anything operators would interpret differently on different shifts

For operator training, I’d avoid teaching the whole SOP word-for-word. Better to train in three layers:

🛠️ **What you do** — PPE, drum, label, fill limit, closure 

📦 **When it changes hands** — when full, move to storage, record weight 
🚨 **What can go wrong** — wrong label, overfilled drum, missing gasket, no torque check

A clean way to handle this is: do one walkthrough with the SOP in hand, redline it, then build a short operator version from the verified process. The full document is for compliance; the operator sheet is for doing the job right at 2 a.m.

1. Walk Through the Process with a Lead Operator: Don't just read the document; actually go to the work area with an experienced operator. As you read each step in the SW007 document (e.g., "Required PPE: Butyl or laminated PE/EVOH gloves"), observe if they are currently doing it that way. This direct observation is invaluable.

2. Ask Targeted Questions: For each section (PPE, Containers, Labeling, etc.), ask the operator:
 * "Is this exactly what we do now?"
 * "Are there any parts here that we do differently?"
 * "Do you see any challenges with following this specific step?"
 "Are the materials or equipment* mentioned (like the specific Uline drum or pallet) readily available and what we currently use?"

3. Focus on Key Changes/New Requirements: Since it's new, highlight areas that might be different from previous practices. For example, if daily waste logging is new, ask how they currently track waste, or if specific torque values for bungs are new, how they ensure proper closure now.

4. Check for Clarity and Completeness: Ask the operator if any steps are unclear or if anything important seems missing from the document. Sometimes, tribal knowledge isn't captured in writing.

5. Document Feedback: Keep a simple notepad or use a digital tool to jot down every piece of feedback, every discrepancy, and every suggested improvement. This will be your basis for giving feedback on the document.

By doing this, you'll not only verify the document but also gain valuable insights from the operators themselves, which will make your eventual training much more effective and relevant! 👷‍ ️📝

5.  **Document Feedback:** Keep a simple notepad or use a digital tool to jot down every piece of feedback, every discrepancy, and every suggested improvement. This will be your basis for giving feedback on the document.

By doing this, you'll not only verify the document but also gain valuable insights from the operators themselves, which will make your eventual training much more effective and relevant! 👷‍♀️📝
